Brief 01 / Problem & scope

The problem was not the part. It was the handoff.

Challenge

Drawings, material certificates, and inspection results lived in separate files. Reviewers compared revisions manually, and exception lots lacked a clear hold-and-release state.

Intervention

Create one lot file: bind each stage to an owner, input evidence, and output record. Treat exceptions as an explicit state rather than an email footnote.

Boundary

This is a website and workflow design demonstration. It does not connect to a real ERP, MES, inspection device, or client. Sample figures illustrate information structure only.

Brief 02 / Workflow

Make every step reviewable.

  1. 1

    Lock the scope

    Confirm drawing revision, material, variant, and critical tolerances.

    Evidence: controlled drawing
  2. 2

    Bind the material

    Link the certificate and incoming check to the manufacturing lot.

    Evidence: material certificate
  3. 3

    Record the first article

    Capture results against a fixed dimensional sheet and flag deviations.

    Evidence: first-article sheet
  4. 4

    Hold exceptions

    Name the reviewer and retain the evidence and next action.

    Evidence: exception state
  5. 5

    Release the lot

    Generate a concise release summary once evidence is complete.

    Evidence: release record

Brief 04 / Limits & next steps

What this sample cannot prove.

  • —

    It cannot prove real production yield, inspection accuracy, or client delivery outcomes.

  • —

    There is no real system connection, automatic notification, database, or server-side audit.

  • —

    A real project would first require data authorization, permission boundaries, and acceptance samples.

What we would instrument next

Measure waiting time, exception causes, and reviewer actions at each stage before deciding what to automate.
